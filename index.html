One piece, start to finish

What happens after somebody hands you a sideboard

Five states, and the software moves the piece through all of them. You make two decisions in the whole run: what it’s worth, and what share the consignor keeps.

  1. Pending

    It comes in

    A neighbour fills in your form with photos, condition, and what they hope to get. It lands in your queue — not your inbox, not a text message, not a note on the counter.

  2. Available

    You price it and approve it

    Set the price and the consignor’s share — your shop default, or a different rate for this one person. It goes live on your shop the moment you approve it.

  3. Reserved

    Somebody holds it

    Furniture rarely sells on a card. A buyer can reserve and collect instead — the piece comes off the floor while they arrange a van, and returns automatically if they don’t show.

  4. Sold

    It sells

    Card online or cash in the shop, either way you mark it sold once. The buyer gets their confirmation and pickup details without you writing an email.

  5. Settled

    The consignor gets paid

    The amount owed is recorded at the moment of sale, so changing a price later can never alter what somebody is owed. Pay them however you like; the dashboard tracks what’s outstanding and what’s settled.

Questions

Before you claim

What does “territory-exclusive” actually mean?

One operator per city. When you buy a town it’s removed from the list and we will not sell it again while your account is active. You’re not competing with another ConsignmentBiz shop down the road, because there won’t be one.

Do I need my own inventory to start?

It helps to open with a dozen pieces so the shop doesn’t look empty, but plenty of operators start with what’s in their own garage and let consignors fill the rest. The intake form is designed to do exactly that.

How do consignor payouts work?

You set the percentage the consignor receives — a shop default, or a different rate for a particular person. When an item sells, the amount owed is recorded automatically at that moment, so a later price change can never alter what someone is owed. You pay them however you like; the dashboard tracks what’s outstanding and what’s settled.

Can buyers pay in person instead of online?

Yes — mark an item “reserve and collect” and a buyer can hold it without paying. The piece comes off the shop floor for a few days while they arrange pickup, and returns automatically if they don’t show. Useful for furniture, and for anyone who’d rather hand you cash.

What happens if I stop running the shop?

Nothing automatic. Your site stays up and your data stays yours. If you decide to stop for good, tell us and we’ll release the territory so someone else can take it on — but that only ever happens when you ask.
